# Spanish Numbers 1-100: Complete Beginner's Guide

---

## 🇰🇷 한국어 요약

### 핵심 (Core Concept)
스페인어 숫자 1-100은 영어와 비슷한 패턴을 가지지만, **16-19**와 **21-29**에서 독특한 결합 형태를 보입니다. 30부터는 규칙적인 패턴(십의 자리 + y + 일의 자리)을 따릅니다.

### 구조 (Structure & Patterns)
| 범위 | 패턴 | 예시 |
|------|------|------|
| 1-15 | 개별 암기 | uno, dos, tres... quince |
| 16-19 | dieci- + 일의 자리 | dieciséis, diecisiete... |
| 20-29 | veinti- + 일의 자리 | veintiuno, veintidós... |
| 30-99 | 십의 자리 + y + 일의 자리 | treinta y uno, cuarenta y cinco... |
| 100 | cien | cien |

### 주의사항 (Cautions)
1. **uno**는 남성 명사 앞에서 **un**으로, 여성 명사 앞에서 **una**로 변합니다.
2. 16-29는 **한 단어**로 씁니다 (dieciséis, veintitrés).
3. 31, 41, 51... 91은 **y** 없이 씁니다 (treinta y uno ✓, treinta y uno ✗ → treinta y uno).
4. **cien**은 100 정확히일 때만, **ciento**는 101 이상일 때 씁니다.

### 다음 단계 (Next Steps)
- 100 이상의 숫자 (ciento, doscientos, mil, millón)
- 서수사 (primero, segundo, tercero...)
- 날짜, 시간, 가격 말하기 연습

## Introduction

### What Are Spanish Numbers?

Numbers are essential building blocks of any language. In Spanish, numbers 1-100 follow logical patterns that make them easier to learn than they first appear. Whether you're shopping in Madrid, telling time in Mexico City, or giving your phone number in Buenos Aires, mastering these numbers is your first practical step toward Spanish fluency.

### Why This Matters

- **Daily survival**: Prices, addresses, phone numbers, dates
- **Foundation**: Needed for telling time, discussing age, quantities
- **Confidence builder**: Numbers are concrete and measurable progress

### Quick Comparison to English

| Aspect | English | Spanish |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 11-19 | Unique words (eleven, twelve...) | Pattern-based (once, doce... + dieci-) |
| 21-29 | Twenty-one, twenty-two... | Single words (veintiuno, veintidós...) |
| 31, 41... | Thirty-one, forty-one... | Treinta y uno, cuarenta y uno... (with "y") |
| Gender | No change | **Uno** changes to **un/una** before nouns |

## Core Explanation

### 1-15: The Foundation (Memorize These)

These are unique words with no pattern. **Memorize them first.**

| Number | Spanish | Pronunciation |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| 0 | cero | THEH-roh |
| 1 | uno | OO-noh |
| 2 | dos | dohs |
| 3 | tres | trehs |
| 4 | cuatro | KWAH-troh |
| 5 | cinco | SEEN-koh |
| 6 | seis | seh-ees |
| 7 | siete | see-EH-teh |
| 8 | ocho | OH-choh |
| 9 | nueve | noo-EH-beh |
| 10 | diez | dee-ehs |
| 11 | once | OHN-seh |
| 12 | doce | DOH-seh |
| 13 | trece | TREH-seh |
| 14 | catorce | kah-TOHR-seh |
| 15 | quince | KEEN-seh |

> **Tip**: Notice *diez* (10) → *once* (11), *doce* (12), *trece* (13) — they share the "ce" ending sound.

---

### 16-19: The "Dieci-" Pattern

From 16 onward, Spanish builds on **diez** (10) + the unit. These are written as **one word**.

| Number | Spanish | Breakdown |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| 16 | dieciséis | diez + y + seis → dieciséis |
| 17 | diecisiete | diez + y + siete → diecisiete |
| 18 | dieciocho | diez + y + ocho → dieciocho |
| 19 | diecinueve | diez + y + nueve → diecinueve |

**Key changes:**
- *y* becomes *i*
- *seis* gains accent mark → *séis* (stress shift)
- All one word, no spaces

---

### 20-29: The "Veinti-" Pattern

Similar to 16-19, but based on **veinte** (20). Also **one word**.

| Number | Spanish | Breakdown |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| 20 | veinte | (base) |
| 21 | veintiuno | veinte + y + uno → veintiuno |
| 22 | veintidós | veinte + y + dos → veintidós (accent on ó) |
| 23 | veintitrés | veinte + y + tres → veintitrés (accent on é) |
| 24 | veinticuatro | veinte + y + cuatro → veinticuatro |
| 25 | veinticinco | veinte + y + cinco → veinticinco |
| 26 | veintiséis | veinte + y + seis → veintiséis |
| 27 | veintisiete | veinte + y + siete → veintisiete |
| 28 | veintiocho | veinte + y + ocho → veintiocho |
| 29 | veintinueve | veinte + y + nueve → veintinueve |

> **Important**: *veintiuno* loses the final *-o* before masculine nouns → *veintiún libros* (21 books). Before feminine nouns: *veintiuna casas*.

---

### 30-99: The Regular Pattern (Easy!)

From 30 onward, the pattern is completely regular:
**Tens + y + Units** (written as **three separate words**)

| Tens | Spanish |
|------|---------|
| 30 | treinta |
| 40 | cuarenta |
| 50 | cincuenta |
| 60 | sesenta |
| 70 | setenta |
| 80 | ochenta |
| 90 | noventa |

**Examples:**
- 31 → **treinta y uno**
- 42 → **cuarenta y dos**
- 53 → **cincuenta y tres**
- 64 → **sesenta y cuatro**
- 75 → **setenta y cinco**
- 86 → **ochenta y seis**
- 97 → **noventa y siete**
- 99 → **noventa y nueve**

> **Rule**: Always use **y** (and) between tens and units from 31-99. No exceptions.

---

### 100: Cien vs. Ciento

| Usage | Form | Example |
|-------|------|---------|
| Exactly 100 | **cien** | *cien personas* (100 people) |
| 101-199 | **ciento** | *ciento uno* (101), *ciento veintitrés* (123) |
| Before nouns (any gender) | **cien** | *cien libros*, *cien casas* |
